## Maria — Home Baker / Food Seller

**Demographics:** Female, 35–45 years old, married with kids, lives in Metro Manila or a Tier 1 city
**Business:** Home-based food business (ube cakes, pastries, kakanin, packed meals). ₱80K–₱250K monthly gross revenue. Sole proprietor, not VAT-registered (under ₱3M threshold). Runs everything from her phone.

**Daily Workflow:**
- 6:00 AM — Checks Facebook and Viber for overnight orders
- 7:00 AM — Buys ingredients at the palengke (wet market), pays in cash, gets handwritten receipts
- 8:00 AM–2:00 PM — Cooks and packs orders
- 2:00 PM–5:00 PM — Delivers orders (GrabExpress or personal delivery), collects GCash payments
- 5:00 PM–9:00 PM — Replies to Facebook DMs, takes new orders for tomorrow, counts cash
- End of day — Stuffs receipts in a plastic bag. Maybe writes a number in a notebook. Usually too tired.

**Primary Pain Points:**
1. **BIR compliance terror.** She knows she's supposed to file quarterly (1701Q) but doesn't fully understand what's required. She's heard horror stories about penalties. The anxiety is constant but she avoids dealing with it.
2. **Zero cash flow visibility.** She has no idea if she's profitable on a given week. Revenue comes in via GCash and cash. Expenses are paid in cash at the palengke. The numbers never meet.
3. **Receipt chaos.** Palengke receipts are faded thermal paper. Shopee purchase confirmations are screenshots. GCash payment notifications pile up in her phone. Nothing is organized.
4. **DM overload.** She spends 2–3 hours a night replying to Facebook DMs with the same information (menu, prices, delivery areas, order confirmation). It's exhausting but she's afraid to miss a sale.

**Decision Triggers (what makes her pay for Pro):**
- Seeing one real insight about her business she didn't know ("Kumikita ka. ₱18,400 ang net mo this month")
- A BIR deadline approaching and KA sending a calm, clear reminder with exactly what she needs to do
- Scanning a receipt and seeing it instantly categorized — realizing the plastic bag of receipts could be replaced

**Maria Moment Examples:**
- "Kumikita ka. ₱18,400 ang net mo this month." — She didn't know her net profit. Now she does.
- "Maria, ₱3,200 ang ingredients mo this week — 15% more than last week. Ingredients pa rin ang pinakamataas mong gastos." — She can see where her money goes.
- "BIR 1701Q mo, 5 days na lang. Based sa records mo, ready na yung data mo. Gusto mo i-check?" — The anxiety lifts.

**Tech Comfort:** Fluent with Facebook, GCash, Shopee, Viber. Comfortable with phone cameras. Not comfortable with spreadsheets or desktop apps. Prefers chat-style interfaces.

**Quote that captures her:** "Alam ko kumikita ako, pero hindi ko alam kung magkano talaga pagkatapos ng lahat ng gastos."

## Jose — Online Seller (Shopee/Lazada)

**Demographics:** Male, 28–35 years old, digitally native, Metro Manila or Cebu
**Business:** E-commerce reseller on Shopee and Lazada. ₱150K–₱500K monthly gross. May be VAT-registered or approaching the ₱3M threshold. Has a small home warehouse (bodega).

**Daily Workflow:**
- 8:00 AM — Checks Shopee Seller Center and Lazada Seller Center for overnight orders
- 9:00 AM–12:00 PM — Packs orders, prints waybills, schedules courier pickups
- 1:00 PM–3:00 PM — Processes returns, updates inventory, reorders stock
- 3:00 PM–6:00 PM — Manages listings, replies to buyer messages on platforms
- Evening — Reviews the day's sales numbers, reconciles GCash/bank deposits, plans next day

**Primary Pain Points:**
1. **GCash income reconciliation nightmare.** Shopee pays him via GCash and bank transfer in batches, often delayed. Matching these batch payments to individual orders is a manual, error-prone process that takes hours weekly.
2. **VAT complexity.** He's approaching or has crossed the ₱3M threshold. VAT registration, the 12% output tax calculation, input tax credits from supplier receipts — it's all confusing and his accountant only helps at filing time.
3. **Multi-platform chaos.** Inventory, pricing, and orders across Shopee and Lazada don't sync. He's manually updating both. Mistakes lead to overselling.
4. **Thin margins, high volume.** His per-unit margins are small. A few wrong pricing decisions or uncounted costs (packaging, courier fees, returns) can silently eat his profit.

**Decision Triggers:**
- Seeing automatic categorization of GCash payments matched to Shopee orders
- Getting a clear VAT calculation based on actual tracked data
- Accurate margin calculation per product that accounts for platform fees and returns

**Tech Comfort:** High. Uses spreadsheets (Google Sheets), comfortable with dashboards and data. Would appreciate API integrations but can work with manual export/import.

**Quote:** "Every month I'm supposed to compute my VAT and I just guess. My accountant fixes it at year-end and charges me extra."

## Ana — Freelance Creative

**Demographics:** Female, 25–30 years old, single, Metro Manila
**Business:** Freelance graphic designer / social media manager. ₱40K–₱120K monthly gross. Registered as a professional under the 8% flat tax option.

**Daily Workflow:**
- Flexible hours — works project-based, often late nights
- Manages 3–5 clients simultaneously
- Invoices monthly, payment terms vary (some immediate GCash, some 30-day bank transfer)
- Tracks time loosely in a notes app or not at all
- Files quarterly using the 8% flat tax option

**Primary Pain Points:**
1. **Invoice tracking chaos.** She sends invoices via email or Canva-designed PDFs. Tracking who has paid, who's overdue, and how much she's owed requires checking multiple GCash and bank notifications manually.
2. **8% flat tax confusion.** She chose the 8% option because it's "simpler" but isn't sure she's computing it correctly. She doesn't know exactly which expenses are deductible (answer: none under 8% flat tax, but she doesn't know that).
3. **Income variability anxiety.** Some months she earns ₱120K, others ₱40K. She has no visibility into her pipeline or average monthly income trend, which makes budgeting and tax estimation stressful.
4. **Late payments.** She's owed money by clients but feels awkward following up. A gentle automated reminder would save her the social discomfort.

**Decision Triggers:**
- Invoice tracking that shows who owes her what, with gentle follow-up drafts
- Clear 8% tax computation that removes the guessing
- Monthly income trend visualization

**Tech Comfort:** High. Designer, so she appreciates good UX. Familiar with Canva, Google Workspace, various SaaS tools. Expects polished UI.

**Quote:** "I'm pretty sure I'm doing my taxes wrong but it's the 'simple' option so how wrong can it be?"

## Andoy — Sari-Sari / Micro-Retail Owner

**Demographics:** Male, 40–55 years old, lives in a barangay (neighborhood), lower digital literacy
**Business:** Sari-sari store (neighborhood convenience shop). ₱30K–₱80K monthly gross. Many transactions are ₱5–₱50 each. Cash-dominant with some GCash. Not BIR-registered (below threshold) but should be.

**Daily Workflow:**
- 5:00 AM — Opens the store window
- All day — Serves customers one at a time, makes change from a cash box
- Afternoon — Walks to the wholesaler to restock items that sold out
- Evening — Counts remaining cash, compares to what he started with, estimates if he "earned" today
- End of day — No records kept. Maybe a notebook with a few numbers. Mostly memory.

**Primary Pain Points:**
1. **Daily cash flow mystery.** He starts the day with cash in the box and ends with cash in the box. He doesn't know his actual profit because he doesn't track individual transactions or separate business cash from personal cash.
2. **Inventory costing blindness.** He buys sardines at ₱14/can from the wholesaler and sells at ₱18/can. But he doesn't account for transportation, spoilage, or the items he gives on credit (utang) that never get paid back.
3. **Utang (credit) tracking.** Neighbors buy on credit. He tracks it in his head or on a piece of paper taped to the wall. He writes off significant amounts monthly because he can't remember who owes what.
4. **Low digital literacy.** He has a smartphone (hand-me-down from a child) and can use Facebook and GCash for receiving payments, but typing is slow and he's uncomfortable with complex apps.

**Decision Triggers:**
- A simple daily "how much did you earn today" check-in that takes 60 seconds
- Seeing his actual daily profit for the first time
- Voice input in Filipino so he doesn't have to type

**Tech Comfort:** Low. Facebook Lite, GCash (for receiving, rarely sending), Viber for family chat. Prefers tapping over typing. Would benefit enormously from voice input.

**Quote:** "Alam kong kumikita ako kasi may pera pa sa kahon sa gabi. Pero hindi ko alam kung magkano talaga."

## BCG Archetype Mapping (validated March 2026)

The BCG "Heart of Hustle" report (July 2025, n=3,098 MSMEs with DTI) identified four MSME segments that map directly to AKBai's personas:

| BCG Segment | AKBai Persona | BCG Quote | Key Validation |
|------------|---------------|-----------|----------------|
| Food service owners | **Maria** | "Find alignment between love for food and need to earn a living" | Passion-driven but cash-flow blind |
| Retailers | **Jose** | "Most opportunistic, addressing gaps in the market" | Multi-channel, margin-sensitive |
| Sari-sari store owners | **Andoy** | "Put up shop out of necessity" | Dreams of "financial freedom, peace of mind, and to be present with my family" |
| Manufacturers | Future expansion | "Passion meets production" — craft-driven, wants to scale | 54% cite passion, 44% have >₱1M assets |

**Real user voices that validate each persona:**
- **Maria pain (Reddit):** "I've seen her struggle with all the paperworks and processes she needs to do to comply with BIR" — a child posting about their mom's small business
- **Jose pain (BigSeller):** "Managing customer messages across Shopee, Lazada, Facebook, Instagram is one of the clearest pain points Filipino sellers face today"
- **Ana pain (Reddit):** "Very depressing to see it can reach 8k for the penalties" — freelancer on missed tax filings
- **Andoy pain (BCG):** "To grow my business to a point where I can hire people without worrying about if I can pay them — because I know the income can support it."

## Persona Priority for Phase 1

| Priority | Persona | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| 1 | **Maria** | Primary target. Largest segment, highest willingness to pay, clearest Maria Moment. Every Phase 1 feature is built for Maria first. |
| 2 | **Jose** | Secondary. Higher revenue but more complex needs (VAT, multi-platform). Phase 1 serves him partially; full fit comes in Phase 2 with GSheets export and Business tier. |
| 3 | **Ana** | Tertiary. Simpler tax situation (8% flat) but smaller market. Invoice Cards serve her well. Full fit in Phase 2. |
| 4 | **Andoy** | Future. Lowest digital literacy, smallest transaction sizes, needs voice-first UX. Phase 3 target when voice input and Daily Check-In are mature. |

When writing user stories in Phase 1, default to Maria unless another persona is specifically relevant. When in doubt, write it for Maria first, then note how the feature adapts for Jose/Ana/Andoy.
