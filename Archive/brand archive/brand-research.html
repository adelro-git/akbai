01 — Brand Archetype & Persona

The Sage-Caregiver Hybrid

Your AI partner that combines deep knowledge with genuine warmth — the smart ate/kuya who always has your back.

Primary Archetype

The Sage

Knows the BIR calendar by heart. Understands costing formulas. Can parse a GCash screenshot in seconds. The Sage archetype drives trust — users rely on it because it's demonstrably competent. It doesn't guess; it calculates, cites, and shows its work.

Secondary Archetype

The Caregiver

Warm, proactive, uses your first name. Sends morning briefings not because you asked, but because it cares about your day going smoothly. The Caregiver archetype drives retention — users come back because the AI feels like a real partner, not a cold tool.

The Persona: "Kai" — Your AI Katuwang

Voice

Taglish-fluent. Switches between Filipino and English naturally, just like your barkada. Calls you by your first name. Uses "po" when appropriate but never sounds stiff.

Competence

Never says "I'm not sure." Instead: "Based sa records mo, here's what I found..." Always shows data, never hand-waves. Confirms before saving anything financial — trust through transparency.

Warmth

"Good morning, [Name]! 3 tasks mo today, 1 BIR deadline sa Friday." The proactive morning briefing sets the tone — this AI cares about your hustle succeeding.

02 — Target Audience

Digital-First Filipino MSMEs

Not sari-sari stores. These are digitally literate entrepreneurs who already live on GCash, Canva, and IG — they just need help with the back-office chaos.

Persona A

The IG/TikTok Seller

Online resellers, thrift shops, dropshippers. Manages 50+ DMs daily. Ships via Lalamove/J&T. Biggest pain: the "HM po?" flood and tracking GCash payments manually. Earns ₱20K–₱100K/month.

Persona B

The Home-Based Food Seller

Bakers, meal preppers, kakanin sellers. Hands literally covered in flour. Can't type. Needs voice input, costing calculators, and BIR compliance as revenue scales past hobby-level. Earns ₱15K–₱80K/month.

Persona C

The Freelance Creative

Designers, VAs, editors, writers. Earns via GCash/PayPal. Needs expense tracking, 8% flat tax monitoring, and client communication polish. Terrified of BIR. Earns ₱25K–₱150K/month.

Persona D

The Small Agency Owner

2–5 person teams doing social media, events, or consulting. Needs task prioritization, financial oversight, and a true AI EA. Most advanced persona — likely Business tier. Earns ₱80K–₱250K/month.

Visual Landscape — Category Aesthetics

Most PH fintech/MSME tools use: blue (#0057B8 range), green (financial trust), clean/corporate sans-serif, white backgrounds, stock photography. This creates visual sameness. Project KA should break from this with warm gradients (coral/mango), dark-first UI (mobile readability), and a conversational visual language (chat bubbles, not dashboards).

04 — Color Direction

Warm Coral & Mango + Cool Teal

Coral-to-mango gradient as primary — warmth, energy, Filipino sunset vibes. Teal as supporting — trust, clarity, financial competence. Gold as accent — achievement, value.

Coral → Mango (Primary)

Warmth, approachability, energy. Filipino sunset and food culture associations. Breaks from the cold blue/green of PH fintech. Signals: "This is different. This is for you."

Teal (Trust)

Competence, clarity, financial reliability. Used for financial data, confirmations, and success states. Balances the warmth of coral with professional credibility.

Gold (Accent)

Achievement, value, celebration. Used sparingly for milestones, premium features, and positive reinforcement. "Your sales hit ₱100K this month!" moments.

Why Dark-First UI?

Practical Reasons

Filipino digital sellers often work late into the night packing orders. Dark mode reduces eye strain. Mobile PWA on dark backgrounds extends battery life on OLED screens (common in PH mid-range phones). Chat interfaces naturally feel more intimate on dark backgrounds.

Brand Reasons

Dark backgrounds make the coral/mango gradient pop. It signals premium and modern — differentiating from the white-background "corporate tool" aesthetic of competitors. It feels like a personal assistant app, not an accounting dashboard.
